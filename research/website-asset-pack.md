## 1. What the public evidence says

A public founder profile describes SheBelieves.pk as an Islamabad-based, community-driven platform dedicated to empowering young females in Pakistan through sports, leadership, and arts, with an emphasis on a safe and inclusive environment, confidence, self-expression, communication, and connection.[1]

Publicly indexed founder activity references a girls’ hiking date with hiking, games, networking, and snacks; a nature retreat; and a vision-board workshop.[1][2]

A third-party post describes it as a girls-only Islamabad community that organizes paid activities such as hiking and horse riding.[4]

A panel post also frames the initiative around young female leaders and women in sport.[3]

**Important verification gap:** one public profile uses the name **Imama Arif**, while a separate panel post credits **Imama Qureshi and Meeral Qureshi**.[1][3] The final site must confirm founder names, titles, headshots, and story before publishing.

**Domain check:** `shebelieves.pk` did not resolve on 14 August 2026. This does not prove the domain is available; ownership and registrar status must be checked before the site is built.

**Instagram limitation:** Agent Reach’s supported Instagram route requires OpenCLI with a logged-in desktop Chrome session. This VPS has no active Instagram backend, so the private/live feed was not scraped and no follower counts, bio copy, testimonials, or visual identity were invented.

## 2. Market research synthesis

### Theme 1 — The desire exists; access does not
**Confidence: High**

In a study of 687 female students across four Pakistani cities, 92.1% described sport as important or most important, while 73.5% reported no physical-activity participation.[5] The website should therefore sell **access, confidence, and belonging**, not lecture visitors about why movement matters.

**Website implication:** lead with a concrete invitation: what is happening, who it is for, why it feels safe, and how to join.

### Theme 2 — Safety, privacy, family trust, and social norms are product features
**Confidence: High**

Research identifies gender bias, parental preference, cultural expectations, facilities, clothing/modesty concerns, and distrust of women’s outdoor activity as material barriers.[5] Another study found religious belief itself was not a negative predictor of participation; only 3.9% of its sample considered religion an obstacle, while cultural and environmental interpretation remained important.[6]

**Website implication:** a “Safety & Parents” page is not legal filler. It is conversion infrastructure. Each event needs visible information about organizers, age range, difficulty, transport, supervision, privacy, emergency process, consent, and what the fee includes.

### Theme 3 — The strongest whitespace is between an academy and a social outing
**Confidence: Medium-High**

The Karishma Ali Foundation positions around sport, education, social change, and marginalized communities.[7]

Right To Play operates large education, life-skills, and child-protection programmes with schools and partners.[8]

Empower Sports Academy presents a high-performance path toward elite athletes, scholars, and leaders.[9]

Hiking and Trekking Club Islamabad competes for outdoor attention through mixed-audience hikes, membership, trips, gear, and safety claims.[11]

**Strategic gap:** SheBelieves.pk can own the approachable middle:

> **Women- and girls-only experiences in Islamabad where movement, creativity, and leadership turn strangers into a confident community.**

This is different from elite training, institutional development programmes, and generic adventure tourism.

### Theme 4 — Community is the result, not decoration
**Confidence: Medium**

Public SheBelieves.pk references repeatedly combine activity with networking, friendship, and like-minded participants.[1][2][4] The offer is not “a hike plus snacks.” It is a socially safer way to try something new and belong.

A search-indexed Express Tribune report described strong participation in a separate Islamabad women-only recreation initiative, but its source page was blocked by bot verification during this research; treat the reported figures as a lead to validate, not a website claim.[10]

**Website implication:** show welcome rituals, first-timer support, buddy systems, group moments, and repeat participation—not only action shots.

### Research limits
- No interviews with founders, participants, parents, or partners.
- Instagram feed and profile metrics were inaccessible through Agent Reach on this VPS.
- No verified prices, attendance counts, safety record, conversion data, testimonials, or legal status.
- Research studies are useful directional evidence, not proof of SheBelieves.pk’s own impact.
- The founders must validate the target ages, geographic reach, business model, and programme names.

## 3. Strategic positioning

### Recommended category
**Girls’ and women’s community experiences**

This is simpler and broader than “sport-for-development,” while still allowing sport, outdoors, leadership, creativity, and wellbeing.

### One-line positioning
**SheBelieves.pk creates girls- and women-only experiences in Islamabad where movement, creativity, and leadership build confidence and real connection.**

### Brand promise
**You can show up as a beginner and leave feeling braver, more connected, and ready for the next experience.**

### Three pillars
1. **Move** — hikes, sport, outdoor challenges, riding, beginner sessions.
2. **Grow** — workshops, leadership practice, communication, panels, creative expression.
3. **Belong** — friendships, networking, community circles, retreats, repeat experiences.

### What the brand is not
- Not an elite sports academy.
- Not an unmoderated meetup group.
- Not a therapy or clinical mental-health service.
- Not a generic “women empowerment” content page.
- Not a national-impact NGO unless legal status and proof support that claim.

### Messaging hierarchy
1. A safe, women-only way to join real experiences.
2. Beginner-friendly detail and a clear next step.
3. Confidence and friendships through doing, not motivational slogans.
4. Founder and facilitator credibility.
5. Verified participant stories and partner proof.

## 4. Audience and jobs to be done

### Primary: first-time participant
**Profile:** girl or young woman in Islamabad/Rawalpindi; curious about outdoor, sport, or creative experiences; may attend alone; may not identify as athletic.

**Job:** “Help me try something new without feeling unsafe, judged, or out of place.”

**Needs on the page:** women-only confirmation, age range, difficulty, photos of real participants, first-timer guidance, price, inclusions, transport/meeting point, and contact access.

### Secondary: returning community member
**Job:** “Give me a reliable rhythm of experiences and people I want to keep coming back to.”

**Needs:** calendar, priority access, membership/waitlist, community benefits, referrals, volunteering, and progress stories.

### Influencer/buyer: parent or guardian
**Job:** “Help me decide whether this organizer, venue, group, and activity are safe for my daughter.”

**Needs:** named team, female supervision, consent process, emergency contact, transport plan, participant privacy, event timing, exact inclusions, and an organizer contact.

### Partner: school, university, venue, brand, coach, or sponsor
**Job:** “Show me a credible programme, a defined audience, a safe delivery model, and a concrete way to contribute.”

**Needs:** partnership formats, audience, programme concept, safeguarding, previous activities, proof, deliverables, and inquiry form.

Use one umbrella word on the site: **Experiences**. “Programmes,” “events,” and “workshops” can be supporting labels.

### Move experiences

- Beginner sport tasters
- Horse-riding sessions
- Trail walks and outdoor days
- Team games and movement challenges

### Grow experiences
- Vision-board workshops
- Leadership and communication workshops
- Women-in-sport panels
- Creative expression sessions
- Mentor conversations

### Belong experiences
- Nature retreats
- Community circles
- First-timer socials
- Member meetups
- Volunteer-led gatherings

**Confirm:** which of these are currently active, who facilitates them, participant age bands, capacity, pricing, and safety requirements.

### Recommended future offer ladder
1. **Single Experience** — low-risk first event.
2. **Community Pass** — priority access or multi-event bundle.
3. **School/University Experience** — hosted programme.
4. **Partner/Sponsor Package** — sponsor seats, venue, gear, facilitator, or a series.

Do not launch all four at once. The v1 site only needs event registration + community waitlist + partner inquiry.

## 17. Case-study framing for Ahsan Khan

### Challenge
A real community exists on social platforms, but public information is fragmented and the website trust layer is missing.

### Method
1. Use Agent Reach to identify first-party and public evidence.
2. Separate verified facts from assumptions.
3. Research the participation barriers and local alternatives.
4. Find the strategic whitespace.
5. Turn it into positioning, architecture, copy, design, forms, proof requirements, and launch gates.
6. Refuse to invent metrics, testimonials, team identity, or safety claims.

### Proposed case-study headline
**From an Instagram handle to a trustworthy community website: the SheBelieves.pk research-to-architecture sprint**

### Story arc
**Signal:** meaningful activities and a clear mission already exist.  
**Problem:** fragmented public information forces participants and parents to trust DMs.  
**Insight:** the real product is safe access + belonging, not individual events.  
**System:** Move, Grow, Belong + event detail + safety + proof + partner paths.  
**Outcome to measure:** registration completion, attendance, repeat participation, and partner support.

### Ethical boundary
Publish the case study only with founder permission if it uses their logo, screenshots, identifiable people, private metrics, messages, or unpublished business information. A public-research methodology can be shown without presenting SheBelieves.pk as a client.
